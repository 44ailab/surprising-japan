Autumn Leaves in Japan: Koyo, the Country's Second Flower Season

Japan has two flower seasons. The famous one is pink and lasts a week in spring. The second one is red, lasts for months, and moves down the country like a slow wave: koyo, the autumn leaves.

When

The colour starts in the mountains of Hokkaido around mid-September and travels south week by week. Kyoto and Tokyo usually reach their peak from late November into early December. Just like the cherry blossom forecast in spring, the news follows a "koyo front" on the weather maps, and people plan trips around the predicted peak the same way.

What happens

The custom is called momijigari — literally "maple hunting." Nobody hunts anything; people travel to famous spots to stand under the trees: temple gardens in Kyoto, the valleys of Nikko, mountain paths lined with Japanese maples. The star is the maple, whose leaves turn a deep, almost glowing red, with ginkgo trees adding bright yellow beside them.

In the evenings, many temples and gardens hold special night openings during the peak, lighting the maples from below so the colour floats in the dark. For a few weeks, the whole country arranges its weekends around trees.

Spring blossoms are loved because they fall quickly. Autumn leaves are loved more slowly — the season is long, the front moves, and if you miss the peak in one place, you can chase it to the next. It is the same national habit of watching nature on schedule, at a gentler speed.

Good to know

In Japan, you can also eat the symbol of the season. In Minoh, near Osaka, maple leaves are pickled in salt for a year, then fried in a sweet batter and sold as momiji tempura — crisp, edible autumn leaves. On Miyajima island, the famous souvenir is momiji manju, a maple-leaf-shaped cake filled with sweet red bean paste.

The depth of the colour depends on the weather: cold nights make the reds stronger, so a warm autumn means a duller year, and everyone says so. Famous Kyoto spots become extremely crowded at peak — the leaves are free, but you will share them with thousands of fellow hunters.
