Culture Day in Japan: Bunka no Hi

On November 3, the museums throw open their doors for free, school gyms fill with student orchestras and food stalls, and the Emperor hands Japan's highest cultural honour to a handful of artists and scientists. It is a holiday with an unusual birthday: it celebrates a constitution.

When

Culture Day, Bunka no Hi, falls on November 3 every year — a fixed date, not a Monday holiday. The date carries two histories at once. It was originally the birthday of the Emperor Meiji, one of the great holidays of prewar Japan. Then, on November 3, 1946, Japan's postwar constitution was officially promulgated, and when the new holiday law was written in 1948, the day was reborn as Culture Day: a day, in the law's words, to love freedom and peace and to promote culture.

What happens

The centrepiece is the Order of Culture, Bunka Kunsho — one of Japan's highest honours, presented by the Emperor himself at the Imperial Palace each November 3 to people who have made outstanding contributions to art, literature, science, or technology. The recipients, usually elderly masters of their fields, appear on the evening news in formal dress.

For everyone else, the day belongs to local culture. Many national museums and art galleries offer free admission. And around this date, schools across Japan hold their bunkasai, culture festivals: classrooms turned into cafés and haunted houses, stages with student bands and plays, corridors lined with calligraphy and science projects. At universities the festivals are open to the public and draw crowds like street fairs — for many students, it is the biggest weekend of the school year.

There is one more thing November 3 is famous for: the weather. It is known across Japan as a hare no tokui-bi, a date with a special talent for sunshine — November 3 in Tokyo is statistically one of the most reliably clear days of the entire year, and the forecast is treated as almost a promise.

Good to know

If you are in Japan in early November, Culture Day is one of the best days of the year to be a tourist: major museums free, university festivals open to everyone, and fine weather more likely than not. University festivals usually run across the weekend nearest the holiday, so check dates — some start in late October. Arrive at popular free museums early; the whole country has the same idea.
