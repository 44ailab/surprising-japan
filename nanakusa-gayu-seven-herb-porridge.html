Nanakusa-gayu: The Seven-Herb Porridge Japan Eats on January 7

After a week of lacquered boxes, sweet beans, rice cakes, and celebration sake, Japan sits down on January 7 to the plainest meal of the year: a bowl of white rice porridge with seven green herbs chopped into it, and almost nothing else. No broth fireworks, no garnish, barely any salt. This is nanakusa-gayu, and its plainness is the entire point. It is the meal a whole country eats to let its stomach rest.

When

January 7 is Jinjitsu no Sekku, the Festival of Humanity, the first of the year's five seasonal festivals. In the old Chinese custom behind it, the first days of the year belonged one by one to different creatures, and the seventh day was humanity's — a day for wishing people health. In Japan it became the day of the seven herbs, and it closes the New Year season: in most of the east, the decorations come down on this day too.

What happens

The seven herbs of spring are seri (water dropwort), nazuna (shepherd's purse), gogyo (cudweed), hakobera (chickweed), hotokenoza (nipplewort), suzuna (turnip), and suzushiro (daikon radish). They are the first fresh greens of the year, and children learn their names as a rhythm, chanted like a tongue twister: seri, nazuna, gogyo, hakobera, hotokenoza, suzuna, suzushiro. The herbs are chopped — tradition says on the night before, or the morning itself — and stirred into a simple rice porridge.

Nobody pretends this is a feast. The porridge is a wish for a year without illness, and at the same time a piece of household engineering: osechi food is rich, salty, and sweet by design, and after days of it, a warm, soft, green-flecked bowl is exactly what a body is asking for. Supermarkets understand the assignment — in the first days of January they sell the seven herbs together in one small plastic pack, so no one has to forage for cudweed.

Good to know

If you are in Japan in early January, nanakusa-gayu is one of the easiest customs to join: the herb packs sit by the vegetables, the porridge takes minutes, and there is no ceremony to get wrong. Eat it on the morning of the 7th, wish yourself a healthy year, and notice what the whole country is doing at the same moment — recovering, quietly and together, from its own celebration.
