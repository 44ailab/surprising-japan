Valentine's Day and White Day in Japan: Chocolate in February, Payback in March

Valentine's Day in Japan runs in reverse. On February 14, it is women who give chocolate to men. And exactly one month later comes White Day, when the men are expected to return the favour — traditionally, three times over.

When

Valentine's Day is February 14, and White Day is March 14. From January, department stores open huge chocolate fairs, with famous chocolatiers from Japan and abroad, and the whole country starts sorting its feelings into price ranges.

What happens

The chocolate comes in categories, and everyone knows them. Honmei choco is the real thing, given to the person you truly like — often handmade, because effort is part of the message. Giri choco is "obligation chocolate," given to bosses, colleagues, and classmates because the day says so, not because the heart does. There is also tomo choco, exchanged between friends, and a growing favourite, jibun choco — good chocolate you buy for yourself.

Then, on March 14, the men answer. The old rule of thumb was sanbai-gaeshi: a return gift worth about three times what you received. Classic White Day gifts are white chocolate, marshmallows, cookies, and candy — the colour white is the theme of the day.

So February is the month of giving, and March is the month of accounting. A small box of chocolate in February quietly creates an appointment in March, and both sides of the exchange understand the arithmetic perfectly.

Good to know

White Day is a Japanese invention. It began in the late 1970s, when a confectionery company in Fukuoka promoted a marshmallow day for returning Valentine's gifts; it was soon renamed White Day and spread across the country. A holiday designed to sell sweets became a genuine national custom within a few years.

Customs are shifting, though. Obligation chocolate has been fading — some companies have even discouraged it, since it can feel like pressure rather than kindness — while expensive chocolate bought for yourself keeps growing. The categories survive, but what people actually want from the day is changing.
