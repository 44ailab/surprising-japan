Toji: Japan's Winter Solstice of Yuzu Baths and Pumpkin

On the darkest evening of the year, Japan runs a bath, drops whole yellow citrus fruit into it until the water is crowded with them, and eats pumpkin for dinner. This is toji, the winter solstice, and its customs are small, warm, and built on wordplay: a pun in the bathwater, a lucky letter on the plate, and a country turning the corner toward the light.

When

Toji falls around December 21 or 22, the shortest day and longest night of the year. It is one of the twenty-four divisions of the old solar calendar, and unlike the big festivals it has no crowds and no ceremony to attend — it happens at home, in the bath and at the table, on the night the sun is at its weakest and begins, from the next day, to come back.

What happens

The bath is yuzu-yu: whole yuzu, the fragrant yellow citrus at its best in winter, floated in hot water. The custom is held together by a pun — toji, the solstice, sounds like toji, the hot-spring cure — and by a proverb: take a yuzu bath on the solstice and you will not catch a cold all winter. Public bathhouses and hot springs across the country run yuzu baths on the day, the water yellow and steaming with scent; at home, a few fruit in the tub do the same job.

On the table is kabocha, Japanese pumpkin. It keeps from summer into the deep winter, it is full of nourishment when fresh vegetables are scarce, and it carries luck in its other name: nankin, which holds the sound n twice. Foods whose names end in n are solstice luck — nankin, ninjin (carrot), renkon (lotus root), daikon, kinkan (kumquat), even udon — because n sounds like un, fortune, and piling up the letter is a way of piling up the luck at the moment the year turns.

Good to know

Toji is the easiest festival in this book to celebrate from a supermarket: yuzu are sold in nets in December precisely for the bath, and a simmered dish of kabocha with soy and a little sugar is an ordinary weeknight recipe, not a ritual production. Shortest day, hot bath, sweet pumpkin. Japan's darkest night is handled with plumbing and dessert, and it works.
