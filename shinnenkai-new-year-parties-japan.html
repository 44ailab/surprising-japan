Shinnenkai: The New Year Parties That Open Japan's Working Year

In December, Japanese offices drink to forget the year. In January, the same people, in the same restaurants, raise a glass to open the new one. These are shinnenkai, New Year parties, and they are the hinge the working year swings on: the first gathering, the first greetings, the first sight of everyone's intentions for the twelve months ahead.

When

Shinnenkai season runs through January, starting as soon as work restarts after the New Year holiday. Companies hold them, and so do departments, trade associations, clubs, school parent groups, and circles of friends — any group that ended December with a bonenkai tends to open January with its mirror image. The biggest ones, held by associations and industries, fill hotel banquet rooms in the first working weeks of the year.

What happens

The shape is familiar — a course meal, drinks, speeches — but the weather inside the room is different from December. A bonenkai lets its hair down; a shinnenkai buttons its jacket. The senior person gives a New Year address: thanks for the year past, hopes and targets for the year begun. People exchange nenshi no aisatsu, the formal first greetings of the year, table by table. At formal gatherings the sake barrel comes out and its lid is opened with mallets — kagami biraki, the opening ceremony borrowed from the mochi custom — and the sake is shared with the whole room.

That difference in tone is the custom's real content. December's party closes accounts: grudges, hierarchies, and embarrassments are meant to be left in the old year. January's party opens them again, clean. You meet the same boss, the same clients, the same rivals — but the greetings are first-of-the-year greetings, and everyone gets to start the relationship over at zero.

Good to know

If you work in Japan, treat a shinnenkai invitation as the more important of the two parties. The bonenkai is where the year is forgiven; the shinnenkai is where the year is decided — projects get their first airing, and the people who show up get counted as the people who are serious. Arrive, greet everyone properly, and drink to the opening. It is the cheapest good impression of the year.
